Compression settings

Breathing between phrases is normal, and every podcast quietens it afterwards rather than expecting the speaker not to breathe. Breaths are found by character — noise-like, unvoiced, in the gaps — so quiet speech isn't mistaken for one. Turning them down keeps the rhythm of your delivery; removing them entirely tends to sound rushed, which is why ducking is the default.

Export

Scrub the preview to the frame you want, then press Use this frame — here, or the same button in the transport under the preview, which is to hand in every step. It is written into the MP4 as cover art — the covr atom, the same one ffmpeg -disposition attached_pic writes — as a JPEG at the source frame's size, capped at 1920 px on the long edge. It is metadata, so it costs no encoding time and no picture quality. Apple's players and Finder, VLC and Plex show it; plenty of other players and file managers ignore any such tag and just decode a frame instead, so the picture is downloadable too: that is the file to hand a <video poster> or a video site.

How this works & privacy

This tool demuxes your file with MP4Box.js, decodes and re-encodes the video with the browser's WebCodecs API — which routes to your machine's hardware video encoder — and repackages the result with mp4-muxer. Every step runs locally in your browser; your video is never uploaded.

AAC audio is copied through unchanged (remuxed, not re-encoded), so keeping audio costs no extra quality and almost no time. Video with an audio codec other than AAC will have its audio dropped — you'll be told when that happens.

Turning on a volume boost decodes and re-encodes just the audio (the video still passes through the hardware encoder untouched). Auto-boost measures loudness in the human-voice band (~300–3400 Hz) so a quiet voice track is judged on the voice itself, not on a single loud non-voice sound, then rides the gain up to rescue it — up to +36 dB — and automatically backs that off the instant anything gets loud (a music jingle, a shout), so a boost sized for a whisper can't blast a louder passage through the roof. It only ever turns audio up, never down. Manual boost is a flat dB you choose yourself, with no automatic backing-off, through a limiter so it can't clip. The Trim/Settings/Export preview plays the same boost live so you can listen before exporting.

Captions are made on your device by OpenAI's open-weights Whisper speech model (MIT), run with transformers.js (Apache-2.0) on your GPU through WebGPU, or on the CPU without it. The model's weights (and the ONNX Runtime engine) are downloaded from Hugging Face and jsDelivr the first time you generate captions and cached by your browser after that — only the model comes in; your audio is transcribed locally and never sent anywhere. The captions are drawn onto the video frames (“burned in”), so they show in every player and on every site.

Respeaking the narration uses Kyutai’s Pocket TTS — a small speech model whose weights are licensed CC BY 4.0 (© Kyutai), exported to ONNX by KevinAHM (MIT) and run here on ONNX Runtime Web (MIT). It clones a voice from a few seconds of speech, so the tool takes the clearest stretch of your own recording as its reference — nothing to record, and the same microphone and room come with it. The model downloads once (146 MB) and then runs entirely on your CPU; as with captions, only the model comes in. The whole script is spoken in one pass, sentence after sentence, and the picture is then re-timed to it — so there is no join anywhere between generated speech and the recording. Kyutai’s terms prohibit cloning a voice without explicit and lawful consent, which is why this is built for respeaking narration you recorded yourself.
